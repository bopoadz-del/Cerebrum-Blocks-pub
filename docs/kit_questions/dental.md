# Dental Clinics — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · agent / item · adult or paediatric · protocol version · source · date

1 — Local anaesthetic catalogue

· 1.1 [GATE] For each agent you stock (lidocaine, articaine, mepivacaine plain and with vaso, prilocaine, bupivacaine, other) — concentration, vasoconstrictor and ratio, cartridge volume you stock, mg per cartridge as you calculate it, adult MRD (mg/kg), absolute cap (mg), paediatric MRD (mg/kg), minimum age, source — the source recorded as a specific figure (regulator / SPC / textbook edition + year / your own protocol).
· 1.2 [GATE] Your cartridge volume rule — do you calculate on labelled 1.8 mL or on deliverable volume after dead space — and where that rule is written.
· 1.3 [GATE] Whether your protocol states that the same cartridge volume carries different mg loads at different concentrations (2% / 3% / 4%) — and where.
· 1.4 [GATE] For each agent — whether the vasoconstrictor can become the limiting factor before the anaesthetic, and for which patient groups.
· 1.5 [GATE] Your multi-agent cumulative rule — how doses are added across agents in a session, and who tracks the cumulative total.
· 1.6 [GATE] Your rounding rule — cartridge counts down always, and where it is written.
· 1.7 [GATE] The source question — where your MRD values come from, in each case. If a textbook, the edition and year. If a regulator, the specific publication. If a practice protocol, its version and approval date.

2 — Emergency drugs and equipment

· 2.1 [GATE] Your emergency kit inventory — presentation, adult dose, paediatric dose, route, location, expiry check frequency and by whom, and the source of each dose.
· 2.2 [GATE] Who checks the emergency kit, how often, where recorded — as practised, not as policy.
· 2.3 [GATE] Which staff are trained to administer which item, and how the currency of training is tracked.

3 — Radiography

· 3.1 [GATE] Your machine-specific exposure parameters — kV, mA, exposure times for bitewing, periapical, OPG, CBCT, collimation — adult and paediatric — as your practice sets them for your machines.
· 3.2 [GATE] Your justification protocol — who authorises an exposure, and on what grounds.
· 3.3 [GATE] Your recall/interval rules for routine radiographs by caries risk — your practice's rule.
· 3.4 [GATE] Your dose audit process, and what triggers a review.
· 3.5 [GATE] Which exposures require a different paediatric protocol on your machines, and how that is enforced at the machine.

4 — Decontamination and infection control

· 4.1 [GATE] Your autoclave cycle parameters as set on your machines — temperature, hold time, pressure, vacuum/non-vacuum selection — for each cycle type you use.
· 4.2 [GATE] Your daily test (steam penetration / leak), periodic validation regime, and where the records live.
· 4.3 [GATE] Your waterline disinfection regime, and the microbial limit you work to.
· 4.4 [GATE] Your ultrasonic bath test regime, and handpiece lubrication/sterilisation protocol.
· 4.5 [GATE] What makes a cycle fail on your machines, and what happens to the load if it does.
· 4.6 [GATE] Who may release a load for use, and what they must check.
· 4.7 [GATE] How instrument traceability to patient is maintained in your practice — the actual record system.

5 — Implants and surgical

· 5.1 [GATE] Which implant systems you use, and — per system — the drilling sequence by bone density, drill speed, irrigation requirement, minimum and maximum insertion torque, abutment screw torque, healing periods (maxilla, mandible), minimum bone dimensions, safety margins to nerve/sinus/adjacent root.
· 5.2 [GATE] Whether your protocol states that torque values and drill protocols are not transferable between implant systems — and where it says so.
· 5.3 [GATE] Your absolute anatomical limits as your clinicians set them — proximity to IAN, mental foramen, maxillary sinus, adjacent roots.
· 5.4 [GATE] Your mandatory imaging before placement, and who reads it.

6 — Materials

· 6.1 [GATE] Per material — working time, setting time, storage, mix ratio — as your practice uses them (which is IFU-derived, but the point is which products you stock and which IFU version is current in your practice).
· 6.2 [GATE] Which materials your practice has flagged as technique-sensitive in a way that changes outcome if protocol is not followed exactly.

7 — Practice economics

· 7.1 [GATE] Your fee schedule per item — examination/consultation, scale and polish, direct restoration, endo anterior, endo molar, extraction simple, extraction surgical, crown, bridge, implant surgical, implant restorative, ortho course, whitening, hygiene, emergency/out-of-hours.
· 7.2 [GATE] Your chair time per procedure — booked vs actual — and which procedures routinely overrun, by how much.
· 7.3 [GATE] Your lab costs and turnaround by item, and which lab work is on the critical path for an appointment.
· 7.4 [GATE] Your materials cost as a percentage of fee, by procedure type.
· 7.5 [GATE] Your recall intervals by risk category — and whether the clinical interval differs from the commercial recall. If yes, confirm the clinical interval always wins.
· 7.6 [GATE] Your failure and remake rates by procedure, and who bears the cost.

8 — Dosing and calculation rules

· 8.1 [GATE] For each rule — weight-based maximum adult, weight-based maximum paediatric, absolute cap per appointment, which limit applies when both bind, multi-agent cumulative rule, vasoconstrictor limit healthy, vasoconstrictor limit cardiac, rounding rule, weight source (measured / estimated / stated), obese or low-weight adjustment, minimum age by agent — your protocol, source, and approving person, and whether the source is external or internal.
· 8.2 [GATE] How patient weight is obtained for paediatric dosing on your practice — measured, parent-reported, or estimated — and how this is recorded.
· 8.3 [GATE] Whether the calculation is done manually, by chart, by nomogram, or by tool, and what the cross-check is.
· 8.4 [GATE] Who verifies the dose before administration in a paediatric case on your practice.
· 8.5 [GATE] Whether the system may perform the mg/kg chain for your practice, and — if yes — whether it must show every step (weight, mg/kg used, total mg, mg per cartridge, cartridges, rounding). (Recommend yes to both. Confirm.)

9 — Medical history and contraindications

· 9.1 [GATE] Per category (cardiac/recent MI, anticoagulation, bisphosphonates/antiresorptives MRONJ, head/neck radiotherapy, uncontrolled diabetes, pregnancy, known drug allergy, latex allergy, methaemoglobinaemia risk, immunosuppression, prosthetic joint/endocarditis risk, epilepsy, under-16/consent) — your absolute contraindications, relative items, and what each requires — with the source.
· 9.2 [GATE] Which require medical practitioner liaison before treatment, and who initiates it on your practice.
· 9.3 [GATE] Which require a prescriber or principal sign-off.
· 9.4 [GATE] Your antibiotic prophylaxis rule, and which guideline your practice follows.
· 9.5 [GATE] How medical history is updated and re-verified on your practice — the actual frequency and record.

10 — Emergency protocols

· 10.1 [GATE] Per emergency (anaphylaxis, LA toxicity, vasovagal syncope, hypoglycaemia, angina/MI, asthma, seizure, airway obstruction, haemorrhage, needlestick) — protocol location, sequence, who is called, and the escalation trigger.
· 10.2 [GATE] Your responsible clinician on call, and your out-of-hours route.
· 10.3 [GATE] How an adverse event is documented and reported on your practice, and to whom.

11 — Evidence standard

· 11.1 [GATE] Per figure class — anaesthetic MRD, mg per cartridge, emergency drug dose, radiography setting, autoclave parameters, implant torque, prophylaxis regimen, material handling — what your practice accepts as proof, and what it rejects even if it states the number.
· 11.2 [GATE] Minimum acceptable citation per source type on your practice — regulator, SPC, manufacturer IFU, clinical guideline, textbook (edition and year), practice protocol (version and approval date), equipment manual.
· 11.3 [GATE] Mandatory qualifiers your practice requires — anaesthetic dose, mg per cartridge, implant torque, radiography setting, autoclave parameters — with agent+concentration+cartridge+vaso+adult/paediatric for dosing, and system-specific for torque.
· 11.4 [GATE] Your derivation rules — mg/kg × weight → total mg, total mg → cartridges, % → mg/mL, cartridge volume conversion (1.8 → 2.2), summing across agents, scaling adult dose for child, carrying torque between systems, estimating weight — allowed or not on your practice.
· 11.4.2 [GATE] Confirm your step-visibility rule — must the system show every step of the mg/kg chain if it performs it?
· 11.5 [GATE] Your staleness rules per figure — practice protocol, textbook MRD, SPC/IFU figure, autoclave validation, radiography QA, medical history, emergency drug stock — valid for how long, stale immediately on what.
· 11.5.1 [GATE] How your practice knows a protocol is current — version field, approval date, register — or whether it depends on a person knowing.
· 11.5.2 [GATE] Confirm the currency rule — does the system refuse or warn when it cannot verify a protocol is current on your practice?
· 11.6 [GATE] Your conflict rule — when SPC and textbook differ on MRD, when regulator and manufacturer differ, when your practice protocol is more conservative than the SPC (and less) — which governs, and whether the system refuses and reports when sources conflict on a dose.
· 11.6.3 [GATE] Confirm the dose-conflict rule — always refuse and report, never pick.
· 11.7 [GAP] Partial-evidence disclosure wording per situation, and which figures must never be offered as estimates — confirmed against your Section 12 list, likely including every dosing figure.
