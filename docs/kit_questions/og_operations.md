# Oil & Gas Operations — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · asset / tag · parameter · source · date

1 — Deferment economics

· 1.1 [GATE] Your agreed deferment cost figure per situation — full facility shutdown, single train/module down, single well shut in, compression down, export/offtake unavailable, rate reduction per %, flaring instead of export, planned outage per hour, unplanned outage per hour — with source, owner, and revision date against price.
· 1.2 [GATE] Your unplanned vs planned ratio on your assets — the multiple as experienced.
· 1.3 [GATE] Your step costs — the points where an outage stops being linear on your asset (contractual delivery failure, tank tops, well restart, flaring limits, regulatory notification).
· 1.4 [GATE] Your restart cost independent of outage — time to stable, off-spec product, chemical consumption, flaring.

2 — Operating costs

· 2.1 [GATE] Your per-item cost — fuel gas/self-consumption, power import, corrosion inhibitor, scale inhibitor, demulsifier, H₂S scavenger, methanol/MEG, amine make-up, catalyst per charge, produced water handling, chemical disposal/waste, flare gas value and carbon cost.
· 2.2 [GATE] Which of your consumables have a rate tied to production vs fixed.
· 2.3 [GATE] Your long lead consumables that constrain operations if stock runs low.

3 — Maintenance and turnaround

· 3.1 [GATE] Your turnaround interval per unit and what governs each on your asset — regulatory, statutory inspection, catalyst life, or reliability.
· 3.2 [GATE] Your cost set — full facility turnaround (cost and duration), single unit turnaround, major equipment overhaul, emergency contractor mobilisation, specialist crew day rate, crane/heavy lift, scaffolding, NDT inspection campaign, vessel entry preparation.
· 3.3 [GATE] Your cost of one day of turnaround overrun.
· 3.4 [GATE] Your proportion of turnaround scope that is discovery work found on opening.

4 — Operating envelope

· 4.1 [GATE] Your envelope table — separator pressure, separator level, compressor suction pressure, compressor discharge temperature, vessel design pressure/MAWP, pipeline MAOP, minimum design metal temperature, furnace/heater tube skin temp, column differential pressure, tank level high-high, flare header pressure, H₂S in product/gas — normal range, alarm, trip/SIF setpoint, safe operating limit, design limit, source for each.
· 4.2 [GATE] Your terminology for alarm, trip, SOL, design limit — which may be exceeded briefly, which never.
· 4.3 [GATE] Who may authorise operating outside normal envelope but inside SOL on your asset, for how long, and what is recorded.
· 4.4 [GATE] What happens on a SOL exceedance on your asset — incident, deviation, or shutdown trigger.
· 4.5 [GATE] Which parameters on your asset have no operator discretion at all.

5 — Named calculations

· 5.1 [GATE] For each calculation — relief valve sizing/required relief rate, flare load and radiation, depressuring rate and time, MAWP/remaining strength corroded, corrosion rate and remaining life, RBI interval, SIL determination/LOPA, gas dispersion/exclusion distance, fire and explosion loading, hydrate prediction, dew point/water content, well deliverability and drawdown limit, sand production limit, pump/compressor operating window, tank filling rate and overfill protection, ESD valve closure time — which the operating team calculates, and which requires engineering authority on your asset.
· 5.2 [GATE] For corrosion rate and remaining life on your assets — what data is required before a figure may be given, and what happens when the last inspection is out of date.

6 — Operational decision

· 6.1 [GATE] Your priority order when a problem occurs.
· 6.2 [GATE] What makes each option impossible on your asset — continue at rate, reduce rate, run with bypassed protection, defer maintenance, controlled shutdown, emergency shutdown/blowdown, restart after trip, operate with temporary repair.
· 6.3 [GATE] Your hard limits that can never be traded, whatever the production pressure.
· 6.4 [GATE] Your authority matrix — operate outside normal envelope, bypass/override a safety function, extend inspection interval, defer safety-critical maintenance, continue with a failed SCE, restart after ESD, break containment, enter confined space, approve temporary repair, approve MOC.

7 — Permits, isolation and overrides

· 7.1 [GATE] Your permit types and what each requires before issue — hot work, cold work, breaking containment, confined space, electrical isolation/LOTO, working at height, excavation, critical lift, diving, SIMOPS.
· 7.2 [GATE] Your isolation register — whether a permit is blocked until isolations verified, and whether an isolation is blocked from removal until the dependent permit is closed — as it actually works on your asset.
· 7.3 [GATE] Your override register — how a bypassed SIF is recorded, who approves, maximum duration, compensating measures mandatory.
· 7.4 [GATE] Where override/isolation state lives — how anyone reading the P&ID or datasheet would know a safety function is overridden or a relief valve is isolated, and whether it is linked to anything a system could read.
· 7.5 [GATE] Your SIMOPS reconciliation — how concurrent activities are reconciled, who can stop them.
· 7.6 [GAP] Your shift handover record, and what must never be lost across it.

8 — Safety critical elements

· 8.1 [GATE] How SCEs are identified on your asset, and where the register lives.
· 8.2 [GATE] Your performance standard per SCE class — fire & gas detection, emergency shutdown, relief and blowdown, fire water, passive fire protection, escape/evacuation/rescue, containment/primary barrier, ignition control — functionality, availability, reliability, survivability, test frequency, failure = what.
· 8.3 [GATE] What happens when an SCE is found impaired on your asset — maximum time it may remain so, compensating measures mandatory.

9 — Evidence standard

· 9.1 [GATE] Per figure class — design pressure/MAWP, MAOP, relief valve set pressure, SIF setpoint/SIL, corrosion rate/remaining life, exposure limit (H₂S, benzene), inspection interval, isolation boundary — what your organisation accepts as proof, and what it rejects even if it states the number.
· 9.2 [GATE] Minimum acceptable citation per source type — API/ASME/ISO standard, national/regional regulation, P&ID, equipment datasheet/U-1A, HAZOP/LOPA study, SIL assessment, operating procedure, inspection report, MOC record.
· 9.3 [GATE] Mandatory qualifiers your organisation requires — pressure (gauge/absolute, location, design/operating), flow (standard/normal/actual, ref T and P), corrosion rate (inspection date, CML, method), relief set pressure (tag, service, cold set vs operating), exposure limit (TWA/STEL/ceiling), gas concentration (%LEL/%vol/ppm).
· 9.4 [GATE] Your derivation rules — unit conversions, interpolating corrosion rate between inspections, extrapolating remaining life beyond last inspection, applying standard formulas, carrying limits from identical item on same asset, carrying limits from sister facility — allowed or not on your assets.
· 9.5 [GATE] Your staleness rules per figure — P&ID data, corrosion rate/remaining life, relief valve certification, SIF proof test, HAZOP/LOPA study, operating procedure, gas detector calibration — valid for how long, stale immediately on what.
· 9.5.1 [GATE] How your organisation knows a P&ID or procedure is current — revision field, MOC register, document control system — or whether it depends on a person knowing.
· 9.5.2 [GATE] Confirm the live-state rule — no answer about protective functions without checking the override and isolation registers.
· 9.6 [GATE] Your conflict rule — when two acceptable sources disagree, does the system answer with higher-authority and say so, answer with both and flag, or refuse and report? Which classes must always refuse?
· 9.6.2 [GAP] A real conflict of this kind on your asset — the two sources and what happened.
· 9.7 [GAP] Partial-evidence disclosure wording per situation, and which figures must never be offered as estimates — confirmed against your Section 10 list.

10 — Never-estimate

· 10.1 [GATE] Which figures your organisation treats as absolute — MAWP/design pressure, MAOP, relief set pressures and required relief rates, SIF setpoints and SIL ratings, minimum design metal temperature, flash points and autoignition, H₂S/benzene/CO/LEL limits, isolation boundaries and double-block-and-bleed requirements, hot work exclusion radii, confined space entry limits, minimum remaining wall thickness, maximum inspection intervals, ESD valve closure times, fire water demand and duration, escape route capacities, maximum tank filling rates.
· 10.2 [GAP] Which of the above have caught you out, and how.

11 — May-estimate

12 — Refusals

· 13.5 [GAP] Which figures are tag-specific on your asset and never applied to an apparently identical item.
