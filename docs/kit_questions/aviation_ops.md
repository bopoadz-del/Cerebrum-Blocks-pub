# Aviation Operations — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · aircraft / station · basis · contract or manual reference · date

1 — Ground handling and turnaround rates

· 1.1 [GATE] Your handling rate catalogue — full handling per turn, ramp only, pushback/towing, GPU per hour, air start, potable water, lavatory service, de-icing Type I and Type IV, cabin cleaning transit and deep, wheelchair/PRM per pax, extra staff out-of-scope — narrowbody, widebody, regional — with contract or ad-hoc source for each.
· 1.2 [GATE] Which of these are in your handling contract and which are billed ad hoc — per station.
· 1.3 [GATE] Your rate change by station type (hub / focus city / outstation / remote / seasonal) — the multipliers as you apply them.
· 1.4 [GATE] Your rate uplift outside published hours, on public holidays, overnight — the specific multipliers.
· 1.5 [GATE] Which services carry an SLA with penalty or bonus, and what triggers each on your contracts.

2 — Airport, navigation and fuel

· 2.1 [GATE] Your airport charge set per station — landing charge per tonne MTOW, parking per hour/tonne, passenger charge per departing pax, security charge, airbridge, remote stand + bussing, route navigation charge, terminal navigation, into-plane fee, fuel price, slot/ATFM penalty — with the source for each.
· 2.2 [GATE] Which of your stations are materially more expensive than your network average, and by how much.
· 2.3 [GATE] Your authoritative fuel price source per station — contract, index, or invoice — and how often each moves.
· 2.4 [GATE] Where tankering is used on your network, and what governs the decision.

3 — The cost of a delay minute

· 3.1 [GATE] Your agreed cost-per-minute figures — at gate engines off, at gate extended (>60 min), taxi/ground engines running, airborne/holding, cancellation per flight — narrowbody, widebody, regional. If you do not hold agreed figures, say so; it becomes a KNOWN_GAP and the OCC decision rule has to be encoded as judgement-based.
· 3.2 [GATE] How the cost changes as delay lengthens — the step points where compensation becomes payable, where a crew goes out of hours, where a network effect starts.
· 3.3 [GATE] The passenger compensation regime that applies to you — which one, and the extraordinary-circumstances exclusions you actually rely on.
· 3.4 [GATE] Your misconnect cost — rebooking, hotel, meals, duty of care — long-haul vs short-haul as you experience it.
· 3.5 [GATE] Your network-effect rule — how you account for a delay propagating down an aircraft's day. Multiplier, rule of thumb, or judgement — say which.

4 — Named calculations

· 4.1 [GATE] For each calculation — minimum turnaround, declared minimum ground time, fuel plan (trip/contingency/alternate/final reserve/taxi), extra fuel / tankering breakeven, payload–range trade, flight duty period limit, rest requirement, max sectors per duty, crew standby callout time, delay cost of an option, swap vs delay vs cancel cost, D0/D15/A15, completion factor, aircraft utilisation — your inputs, the rule/standard each follows, and who owns it.
· 4.2 [GATE] For minimum turnaround — what actually drives it on your network (longest task, contract, aircraft type) and where it differs by station.
· 4.3 [GATE] For fuel — which elements are regulatory minimums and which are captain's/dispatcher's discretion on your airline, and where that discretion is recorded.
· 4.4 [GATE] For duty limits — which authority applies on your AOC, where commander's discretion can extend, and what must be recorded.

5 — The recovery decision

· 5.1 [GATE] Your recovery option order as the OCC actually considers it — delay, aircraft swap, crew swap, sub-charter, cancel, combine, reroute, divert, ferry, other.
· 5.2 [GATE] What makes each option impossible on your airline — hard blockers, not cost.
· 5.3 [GATE] Your hard limits that can never be traded, whatever the commercial cost.
· 5.4 [GATE] Your authority matrix — cancel, MEL/CDL dispatch, commander's discretion extension, accept defect for onward flight, ferry, divert, off-schedule fuel uplift.

6 — Delay coding

· 6.1 [GATE] Your coding scheme — IATA AHM 730, AHM 731 sub-codes, your own, or AHM 732.
· 6.2 [GATE] Which codes are most often applied wrongly in your operation, and what the correct codes are.
· 6.3 [GATE] Which codes carry a contractual or financial consequence on your network — handler bonus/malus, ATFM attribution, insurance.
· 6.4 [GATE] Who assigns the code, and who can change it after the fact.

7 — Evidence standard

· 7.1 [GATE] Per figure class — weights and balance limits, fuel minimums, performance/runway data, MEL dispatch conditions, duty and rest limits, handling rate/contract charge, airport charge, delay cost figure, compensation entitlement — what your operation accepts as proof and what it rejects even if it states the number.
· 7.2 [GATE] Minimum acceptable citation per source type — ICAO Annex, national regulation, OpSpecs/AOC conditions, AFM/performance manual, OM-A/OM-B, MEL/CDL, IATA AHM, handling contract, airport tariff, OCC standing instruction. State whether "OM-A" is acceptable or if revision + paragraph + effective date is required.
· 7.3 [GATE] Mandatory qualifiers your operation requires — MTOW (registration, configuration, runway, temperature, QNH), fuel minimum (sector, alternate, reserve policy applied), FDP (report time, sectors, acclimatisation, augmentation), handling rate (station, aircraft category, shift, contract vs ad hoc), delay cost (aircraft type, phase, delay band).
· 7.4 [GATE] Your derivation rules — interpolating performance tables, unit conversions (kg/lb, litres/USG), reading off performance charts, applying stated formulas, summing fuel components, carrying figures from a sister aircraft of the same type, carrying rates from a similar station — allowed or not on your airline.
· 7.4.1 [GATE] Confirm the never rules — type-to-registration carry-over and station-to-station rate carry-over as your operation draws them.
· 7.5 [GATE] Your staleness rules per figure — OM/manual, MEL rectification interval, performance data, handling contract rate, airport tariff, fuel price, NOTAM-affected figures — valid for how long, stale immediately on what.
· 7.5.1 [GATE] How the system knows a manual or contract is current on your operation — revision field, effective date, register — or whether it depends on a person knowing.
· 7.5.2 [GATE] Confirm the currency rule — refuse or warn when currency cannot be verified.
· 7.6 [GATE] Your conflict rule — when two acceptable sources disagree, does the system answer with the higher-authority source, answer with both and flag, or refuse and report? Does it change by figure class? Which classes must always refuse — AFM vs OM-B in particular.
· 7.6.2 [GAP] A real conflict of this kind on your operation — the two sources, and what happened.
· 7.7 [GAP] Partial-evidence disclosure wording per situation, and which figures must never be offered as estimates — confirmed against your Section 8 list.

· 8.2 [GAP] Which of the above have caught you out, and how.
