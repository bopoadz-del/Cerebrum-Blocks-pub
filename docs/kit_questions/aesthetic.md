# Cosmetic & Aesthetic Clinics — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · product / procedure · brand · protocol version · source · date

1 — Product catalogue

· 1.1 [GATE] For each neuromodulator you stock — brand, unit basis, vial size, your reconstitution volume, resulting concentration, your storage/stability-after-reconstitution rule — with source.
· 1.1.1 [GATE] Confirm the brand-unit rule for your clinic — are units interchangeable between the products you stock? State it explicitly; it becomes a hard rule.
· 1.1.2 [GATE] Your standard reconstitution volume — one across the clinic, or per indication — and who sets it.
· 1.1.3 [GATE] Your maximum total dose per session, as your protocol states it, and where that is written.
· 1.2 [GATE] For each dermal filler you stock — brand/range, type (HA, CaHA, PLLA, other), syringe volume, reversible or not, typical residence time — with source.
· 1.2.1 [GATE] Confirm which of your products are not reversible — the hard rule.
· 1.2.2 [GATE] Whether different HA brands in your stock dissolve differently, and whether your protocol differentiates.
· 1.3 [GATE] For each energy device you operate — modality, wavelength(s), operator-settable parameters, your manufacturer's limits — with source.
· 1.4 [GATE] For each peel or topical you stock — active, concentration, pH, your max contact time — with source.

2 — Clinic economics

· 2.1 [GATE] Your fee schedule per item — consultation, neuromodulator per area, neuromodulator per unit, filler per syringe/mL, skin booster, laser/IPL per area, peel, thread lift, packages/courses, review, complication management.
· 2.2 [GATE] Your complication management cost — charged, absorbed, insured — and what an occlusion event actually costs you in product, time and follow-up.
· 2.3 [GATE] Your chair time per treatment type — including consultation, consent, photography, treatment, recovery.
· 2.4 [GATE] Your product cost as a percentage of price by treatment type.
· 2.5 [GATE] Your commercial repeat interval versus clinical minimum interval — and confirmation that the clinical minimum always wins where they differ.

3 — Dosing and treatment planning

· 3.1 [GATE] For each rule — max neuromodulator units per session, max units per anatomical area, max filler volume per session, max filler volume per area, reconstitution/dilution standard, hyaluronidase elective, hyaluronidase vascular occlusion, minimum interval between neuromodulator sessions, between filler sessions, between energy device sessions, cumulative annual limits, combination treatment rules (same session) — your rule, the source, and the approving person.
· 3.2 [GATE] For each row above — external authority or your own clinic protocol — marked explicitly. Where yours, who wrote, who approved, when last reviewed.
· 3.3 [GATE] Which protocols have no external standard at all on your clinic, so yours is the only authority. (Elective hyaluronidase is the known case — list any others.)

4 — Energy device settings

· 4.1 [GATE] Per device, per Fitzpatrick group (I–II, III–IV, V–VI) — fluence, pulse duration, spot size, cooling, passes — as you set them on your machines.
· 4.2 [GATE] Which skin types you do not treat with each device — the refusal rule as your clinic sets it.
· 4.3 [GATE] Your test patch rule — mandatory for which devices, which skin types, and how long before treatment.
· 4.4 [GATE] Your absolute maximum settings beyond which the device must not run, regardless of clinical judgement.
· 4.5 [GATE] How settings change on your clinic for tanned skin, recent sun exposure, or active pigmentation.
· 4.6 [GATE] Your eye protection and safety controls — what is mandatory, who verifies before each session.

5 — Screening and contraindications

· 5.1 [GATE] Per category (pregnancy/breastfeeding, known allergy including wasp/bee → hyaluronidase, autoimmune/immunosuppression, neuromuscular disorder, anticoagulation, active infection at site, recent dental work, prior permanent or unknown filler, isotretinoin, keloid history, photosensitising medication, unrealistic expectations / BDD indicators, under 18) — your absolute contraindication, relative items and what each requires — with source.
· 5.2 [GATE] Which of these require prescriber or medical director sign-off before proceeding on your clinic.
· 5.3 [GATE] Your rule for prior filler of unknown type or origin — how it is handled on your clinic.
· 5.4 [GATE] Your psychological screening approach, and what triggers a decline.

6 — Anatomical risk

· 6.1 [GATE] Your high-risk zone classification per zone (glabella, nose, nasolabial, temple, forehead, infraorbital/tear trough, lips, other) — and what that classification changes (technique, product, operator seniority, or refusal).
· 6.2 [GATE] Which zones may only be treated by which level of practitioner on your clinic — as an enforced rule.
· 6.3 [GATE] Whether ultrasound is used on your clinic for guidance or complication assessment, and under what circumstances.

7 — Emergency protocols

· 7.1 [GATE] Your vascular occlusion protocol — sequence from suspicion to resolution, who is called, at what point, threshold for emergency referral.
· 7.2 [GATE] Your vision change / neurological symptoms protocol — immediate action, and confirmation it matches the published escalation rule.
· 7.3 [GATE] Your hyaluronidase availability — where stored, at what temperature, stock level held, who checks expiry, and whether you have confirmed availability at your referral hospital.
· 7.4 [GATE] Your anaphylaxis protocol — what is held, where, who is trained.
· 7.5 [GATE] Your other emergency protocols on file — toxin spread, infection, biofilm, nodule, necrosis.
· 7.6 [GATE] Your medical director on call, and your out-of-hours escalation route.
· 7.7 [GATE] How an adverse event is documented and reported on your clinic, and to whom.

8 — Evidence standard

· 8.1 [GATE] Per figure class — product dose/concentration, reconstitution volume, device settings, treatment interval, contraindication, emergency dosing, storage/stability — what your clinic accepts as proof, and what it rejects even if it states the number.
· 8.2 [GATE] Minimum acceptable citation per source type on your clinic — SPC, IFU, published guideline, regulator requirement, clinic's own protocol (version + approval date), device manual.
· 8.2.1 [GATE] Confirm your own-protocol rule — version and approval date required for every internal protocol citation.
· 8.3 [GATE] Mandatory qualifiers your clinic requires — neuromodulator dose (brand, units, area, reconstitution), filler volume (brand, product line, area, session vs cumulative), device setting (device, skin type, area, pass), hyaluronidase dose (indication, protocol version), interval (which treatment, clinical vs commercial).
· 8.4 [GATE] Your derivation rules — converting units between neuromodulator brands, calculating concentration from vial + diluent, scaling by weight, interpolating device settings between skin types, summing doses across areas, carrying settings between devices, carrying protocols from previous clinics or courses — allowed or not on your clinic.
· 8.4.1 [GATE] Confirm the never rules — brand-to-brand unit conversion, and device-to-device setting carry-over.
· 8.5 [GATE] Your staleness rules per figure — clinic protocol, SPC/IFU figure, device settings, contraindication list, price list — valid for how long, stale immediately on what.
· 8.5.1 [GATE] How your clinic knows a protocol is current — version field, approval date, register — or whether it depends on a person knowing.
· 8.5.2 [GATE] Confirm the currency rule — does the system refuse (recommended) or warn when it cannot verify currency?
· 8.6 [GATE] Your conflict resolution — SPC vs clinic protocol (more conservative and less), published guideline vs IFU.
· 8.6.3 [GATE] Confirm the conflict rule — always refuse and report when sources conflict on a dose, never pick.
· 8.7 [GAP] Partial-evidence disclosure wording per situation, and which figures must never be offered as estimates — confirmed against your Section 9 list, likely most of them.

9 — Never-estimate

· 9.1 [GATE] Which figures your clinic treats as absolute — neuromodulator dose by brand, hyaluronidase dose for occlusion, laser fluence by skin type, peel contact time, reconstitution and dilution, anaesthetic max dose, device fluence/pulse/cooling by skin type, peel concentration/pH/contact time, minimum treatment intervals, cumulative limits, storage temperatures and stability windows, emergency drug doses.
· 9.2 [GAP] Which of the above have caught you out, and how.
