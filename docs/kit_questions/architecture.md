# Architecture Design Office — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · project type / element · basis · source · date

1 — Practice economics

· 1.1 [GATE] Your charge-out rates, true staff cost rates, utilisation target, overhead recovery, target profit margin — junior / mid / senior-director — with source.
· 1.2 [GATE] Your fee % range by project type — residential bespoke, residential volume, commercial office, retail/F&B, hospitality, healthcare, education, industrial/logistics, fit-out, masterplanning, refurbishment — and what moves it up and down on your practice.
· 1.3 [GATE] Your fee distribution by stage — what proportion sits in each work stage, and which stage you most often under-price.
· 1.4 [GATE] Your additional services list and rates — detailed joinery, interiors, landscape, authority liaison, site supervision, as-builts — charged separately, at what percentage or lump sum.
· 1.5 [GATE] Your variation threshold — when does a change become a variation rather than absorbed effort on your practice? Threshold, percentage, revision count, or named trigger.

2 — Production norms

· 2.1 [GATE] Your hourly outputs per deliverable — concept design, schematic/developed, technical/IFC, GA plan, section/elevation, detail, schedule, specification section, BIM LOD 200→300, clash cycle, authority submission pack, tender query response, shop drawing review, site inspection — hours per unit as your office actually produces them, with what changes each.
· 2.2 [GATE] Your assumed revision cycles per drawing at each stage, and what an unplanned extra cycle costs you.
· 2.3 [GATE] Your coordination-time proportion of technical-stage work — how much is consultant coordination vs drawing — as you actually experience it.
· 2.4 [GATE] Which deliverables blow out most often on your projects, and why.

3 — Design benchmarks

· 3.1 [GATE] Your office benchmarks — GIA as % of GEA, NIA as % of GIA, wall-to-floor ratio, façade area per m² GIA, core area %, circulation %, plant area %, parking per unit/100 m², cost per m² shell & core, cost per m² fit-out, sanitary provision, lift provision — with what each varies by.
· 3.2 [GATE] Which benchmarks on your projects are client requirements vs code minimums — the distinction that changes whether a shortfall is negotiation or refusal.
· 3.3 [GATE] Which vary by jurisdiction on your markets, and by how much.

4 — Area and planning

· 4.1 [GATE] Your area-basis definitions and their uses — GEA/IPMS 1, GIA/IPMS 2, NIA/IPMS 3, local authority GFA, saleable/leasable, BUA, plot ratio/FAR, site coverage, setbacks, height limit, balcony/terrace treatment, basement and parking exclusion — with the standard or authority each follows.
· 4.2 [GATE] Which measurement standard governs on your projects — RICS, IPMS, local municipality — and where they conflict, which wins for which purpose.
· 4.3 [GATE] The critical one — which areas count toward plot ratio in your jurisdictions, and which are excluded (basements, parking, plant, balconies, terraces, service areas, double-height voids). Your rules per market.
· 4.4 [GATE] Which area figure goes on planning submission, cost plan, lease, marketing on your projects — and whether they are ever the same number in your practice.
· 4.5 [GAP] A case where an area figure was quoted without its basis and caused a problem.

5 — Compliance calculations

· 5.1 [GATE] Your inputs and method for each calculation — occupancy load, means of escape width, travel distance, number of exits, refuge, compartment size limits, fire resistance by element, smoke ventilation, accessible route gradient/width, accessible WC, lift provision, sanitary provision, parking and accessible bays, daylight factor/autonomy, natural ventilation area, U-values, acoustic separation, structural grid/span coordination — with the standard or authority each follows.
· 5.2 [GATE] Which authority's code applies in each market you work in (SBC, DM/DCD, IBC, Approved Documents, Estidama, other), and where two apply at once.
· 5.3 [GATE] Which of these your office calculates, and which a consultant owns — as a hard boundary.
· 5.4 [GATE] For fire and life safety — at what point it stops being your designer's calculation and becomes a fire engineer's determination on your projects.

6 — Coordination and information

· 6.1 [GATE] Your BIM LOD expectations by stage — what each level actually means in your office.
· 6.2 [GATE] Your information exchange protocol — who issues what to whom, in what format, at what stage — as practised.
· 6.3 [GATE] How clashes are classified and prioritised on your projects, and your tolerance.
· 6.4 [GATE] Your drawing register and revision protocol, and how site knows which revision is current.

7 — Evidence standard

· 7.1 [GATE] Per figure class — code requirement (fire/egress/accessibility), structural dimension or opening, MEP capacity or clearance, floor area, plot ratio/planning constraint, fire rating, product performance (U-value/acoustic), site level/survey data — what your practice accepts as proof, and what it rejects even if it states the number.
· 7.2 [GATE] Minimum acceptable citation per source type — building code/regulation, planning consent condition, fire strategy report, structural drawing, MEP drawing, specification, manufacturer's data/test certificate, survey, client brief. State whether "the building code" is acceptable, or if code + table + clause + edition is required.
· 7.3 [GATE] Mandatory qualifiers your practice requires — floor area (basis, which floors, inclusions/exclusions), travel distance (occupancy class, single/multiple direction, sprinklered), fire rating (element, compartment line, code and edition), level (FFL or SSL, datum, build-up allowance), U-value (element, tested or calculated, standard).
· 7.4 [GATE] Your derivation rules — scaling off drawings, converting GEA↔GIA, interpolating code tables, applying code formulas, summing areas, carrying figures from earlier stages of the same project, carrying from similar previous projects, deriving levels from sections — allowed or not on your projects.
· 7.4.1 [GATE] Confirm the never rules — scaling off drawings and carrying figures from previous projects.
· 7.5 [GATE] Your staleness rules per figure — drawing dimension, code requirement, planning consent condition, fire strategy figure, survey/site level, product certificate, cost benchmark — valid for how long, stale immediately on what.
· 7.5.1 [GATE] How your practice knows a drawing or report is the current revision — revision field, issue register, CDE status — or whether it depends on a person knowing.
· 7.5.2 [GATE] Confirm the currency rule — refuse or warn when currency cannot be verified.
· 7.6 [GATE] Your conflict rule — when two acceptable sources disagree, does the system answer with the higher-authority source, answer with both and flag, or refuse and report? Does it change by figure class? Which classes must always refuse (drawing vs specification, architectural vs structural are the common cases).
· 7.6.2 [GAP] A real conflict of this kind on your projects — the two sources, what happened.
· 7.7 [GAP] Partial-evidence disclosure wording, and which figures must never be offered as estimates — confirmed against your Section 8 list.

8 — Never-estimate

· 8.1 [GATE] Which figures your practice treats as absolute — escape travel distances and widths, exit numbers and refuge provision, compartmentation and fire ratings, smoke ventilation, accessible gradients/widths/turning circles, ceiling heights and habitable-room minimums, balustrade heights and gaps, structural openings/spans/loads, MEP clearances and riser sizes, plot ratio/setbacks/height limits, fire-rated glazing limits, sanitary and lift provision, U-values and thermal bridging, acoustic separation.
· 8.2 [GAP] Which of these have caught you out, and how.

9 — May-estimate

· 9.1 [GAP] Figures you accept as estimates — concept-stage GFA, cost per m², stage duration — and the exact wording you want attached.

10 — Refusals

· 10.1 [GATE] Your list of questions the system must refuse outright — does this design comply, can I remove this wall, is this fire strategy acceptable, will planning approve this — and what the system should say instead.

11 — Precedence and boundaries

· 11.1 [GATE] Your ranking, highest first — building code/regulation, planning consent conditions, contract documents, specification, structural drawings, MEP drawings, architectural drawings, consultant reports, contractor shop drawings, sketches/markups.
· 11.2 [GATE] Drawings vs specification on your projects — which governs, and whether the contract form decides it.
· 11.3 [GATE] Discipline boundaries — which figures must never be taken from the architectural set on your projects, even though they appear on it. Structural member sizes, riser and plant space dimensions, site levels, and any others you name.
· 11.4 [GATE] How someone knows a drawing is the current revision on your practice, and what happens when a superseded one is built to.
· 11.5 [GATE] Jurisdiction — which codes apply where you work, what changes between them, and where a clause has been applied from the wrong code, occupancy class, or building-height band.
· 11.6 [GAP] Which figures are project-specific on your practice and never carried to another project (consent conditions, fire strategy assumptions, approved deviations, client-specific standards).

12 — Unit-confusion history

· 12.1 [GAP] Where an area basis confusion has caused a real problem on your projects — a lease, a submission, a cost plan.
· 12.2 [GAP] Where an FFL vs SSL error has caused one on yours.

13 — Incidents

· 13.1 [GAP] A dimension or level taken from the wrong drawing set or a superseded revision.
· 13.2 [GAP] A code clause applied from the wrong jurisdiction, occupancy class, or height band.
· 13.3 [GAP] An area figure quoted without its basis, and what followed.
· 13.4 [GAP] A figure right at concept and wrong by the time it was built.
· 13.5 [GAP] A coordination clash that traced back to a number rather than geometry.
· 13.6 [GAP] Something taken from a previous project that did not apply to this one.
· 13.7 [GAP] A submission refused on a figure rather than on design.
· 13.8 [GAP] What a Part 1 or graduate architect most commonly gets wrong in their first year.

14 — The five questions

· 14.1 [GAP] Five things you'd ask a system per day and trust completely.
