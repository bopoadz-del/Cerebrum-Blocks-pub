# Heritage Building Restoration — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · building / element · grade · source · date

1 — Materials catalogue

· 1.1 [GATE] Your mortar catalogue as actually specified — binder type, class, mix ratio (volume or weight, stated), compressive strength, vapour permeability, and the substrate each must remain weaker than — with the source of each specification.
· 1.1.1 [GATE] Your rule for maximum permissible repair strength relative to substrate — ratio, tested value, or judgement — and how it is applied.
· 1.1.2 [GATE] What determines lime selection on your projects — exposure, substrate, season, availability — and where availability has forced a compromise, and who authorised it.
· 1.1.3 [GATE] When mortar analysis is mandatory on your projects before specification, and what it tests for.
· 1.1.4 [GATE] Your organisation's position on cement on historic fabric — where permitted, under whose authority, and where never.
· 1.2 [GATE] Your masonry / timber / fabric catalogue — sourcing, matching criteria, lead times, costs, for stone (quarry and reclaimed), brick, terracotta, structural timber, joinery timber, lath, glass, lead, slate, tile, ironwork, paint.
· 1.2.1 [GATE] For stone matching on your projects — which criteria are non-negotiable (geology, bed, colour, texture, weathering, porosity).
· 1.2.2 [GATE] Which materials govern your programme rather than the works — your quarry reopening, your handmade lead times.
· 1.2.3 [GAP] What you permit as reclaimed on your projects, what must be new, and where provenance or ethics questions have arisen.

2 — Rates and productivity

· 2.1 [GATE] Your rates per operation — raking out, repointing, indent, dutchman, stone replacement (plain and carved), stitching, grout consolidation, lime plaster, lath, limewash, timber splice, resin repair, joinery repair, leadwork, roof strip, paint analysis/removal, cleaning (poultice/nebulous/laser), recording — with conditions that change each.
· 2.2 [GATE] Your lime working limits as practised — minimum and maximum temperature, frost risk period, protection and curing regime, duration.
· 2.3 [GATE] On your projects, what proportion of programme is investigation and recording vs physical work — and how this is contracted.
· 2.4 [GATE] Your skilled labour availability picture — how far ahead conservation masons, leadworkers, specialist joiners must be booked, per trade.

3 — Cost of getting it wrong

· 3.1 [GATE] Your consent timeline — application to determination — and what extends it on your projects.
· 3.2 [GATE] Cost of scaffold standing while consent, approval or specialist is awaited — your figure.
· 3.3 [GAP] Where removing a previous inappropriate repair cost more than the original work would have — real example.
· 3.4 [GAP] How loss of original fabric is treated commercially on your projects — contractual mechanism, or professional obligation only.
· 3.5 [GATE] Your cost set — unauthorised works (enforcement, prosecution, reinstatement), consent refused after works started, cement pointing removal and redo, programme delay, watching brief delay, specialist re-mobilisation, additional investigation, PI impact.

4 — Conservation principles as operating rules

· 4.1 [GATE] How you establish "necessary" before accepting removal of any original fabric — what evidence required.
· 4.2 [GATE] Which of your standard techniques are genuinely reversible, and which are not — resin repairs, stainless stitching, consolidants, grouts — and what authorises using the irreversible ones anyway.
· 4.3 [GATE] What your compatibility assessment actually tests — strength, porosity, vapour, thermal, salt, chemical.
· 4.4 [GATE] Your convention for distinguishability — tell-tale, date stamp, setback, record only.
· 4.5 [GATE] What you record before, during and after, and where the record goes so a conservator in fifty years can find it.
· 4.6 [GATE] Where these principles conflict with each other on your projects — reversibility vs structural adequacy, distinguishability vs harmony, minimum intervention vs client use — and how you resolve, and by whom.

5 — Consent and statutory scope

· 5.1 [GATE] Your jurisdiction's repair / alteration line — the specific boundary as it applies on your projects, with the basis for each case: repointing matching vs different mortar, stone replacement like-for-like vs different stone, removing later render, removing lime plaster, window replacement identical vs different glazing, adding insulation, coatings, chasing, DPC, cleaning, paint removal.
· 5.2 [GATE] Which consents your projects require and who determines each — listed building consent, scheduled monument consent, planning, conservation area consent, ecclesiastical exemption, building regs, archaeological condition, protected species licence.
· 5.3 [GATE] Your jurisdiction's order of precedence — does heritage compliance sit before or after building regulations? Confirm, because this changes every damp, insulation and ventilation answer.
· 5.4 [GATE] Consent conditions typically attached on your projects, and which constrain construction directly — method statements, sample panels, watching briefs, recording, material approval.
· 5.5 [GATE] Your sample panel process — when required, who approves, lead time, can any work proceed before approval.
· 5.6 [GATE] Your trigger for consent variation rather than site decision — discovery on opening up as the common case.
· 5.7 [GATE] Your emergency works provision — what may be done without prior consent, and what must follow.

6 — Investigation and diagnosis

· 6.1 [GATE] Which investigations you require complete before a specification can be written on your projects, and which may run alongside.
· 6.2 [GATE] Your trigger for opening up, and the authority to do it.
· 6.3 [GATE] How you establish cause vs symptom — rising damp, condensation, penetrating damp, salt efflorescence — your diagnostic method.
· 6.4 [GATE] Your archaeological watching brief trigger and what stops work when something is found.
· 6.5 [GATE] Your investigation catalogue — when mandatory, what each establishes, lead time, who performs — for measured survey, condition survey, mortar analysis, petrography, paint stratigraphy, timber ID, dendro, moisture, salt, structural monitoring, archaeological evaluation, archival research, NDT, species survey.

7 — The intervention decision

· 7.1 [GATE] Your order of options considered for a decayed element — monitor, maintain, consolidate, repair in situ, piece in, partial replace, full replace like-for-like, modern substitute, reconstruction.
· 7.2 [GATE] What makes each option impossible on your projects — hard blockers per option.
· 7.3 [GATE] Your absolute prohibitions — things never done to historic fabric on your projects, whatever client pressure or budget.
· 7.4 [GATE] Your authority matrix — remove original fabric, change specified material, depart from method statement, accept substitute on lead time, approve sample panel, instruct emergency works, vary consent, sign off completed work, stop work on find.
· 7.5 [GATE] Your resolution route when two experts disagree — conservation architect vs structural engineer, conservation officer vs client.

8 — Evidence standard

· 8.1 [GATE] Per figure / decision class — mortar mix for this building, substrate strength, what is original fabric, consent scope, structural adequacy, compatibility, historic detail for reinstatement — what your organisation accepts as proof and what it rejects even if it states the number.
· 8.2 [GATE] Minimum acceptable citation per source type on your projects — legislation, national heritage body guidance, standard (part and year), listed building consent, condition survey, materials analysis, archival source, specification.
· 8.3 [GATE] Mandatory qualifiers your organisation requires with each figure — mortar mix (with substrate), "original fabric" (with evidence basis), lime type, consent position, strength value, historic detail.
· 8.3.2 [GATE] Confirm your rule — must every claim about the building's history state whether it is documented, inferred, or assumed?
· 8.4 [GATE] Your derivation rules — applying published mix to this building, inferring mortar visually, carrying spec between elevations, between buildings, inferring lost detail from fragments, from comparators, scaling historic drawings, interpolating strength from material type, assuming phase from style, proposing material on performance grounds — allowed or not on your projects.
· 8.4.2 [GATE] Confirm the inversion rule — never propose a material on performance grounds alone, without compatibility assessment.
· 8.5 [GATE] Your staleness rules per figure — consent, condition survey, materials analysis, monitoring, method statement, species survey, archival research — valid for how long, stale immediately on what.
· 8.5.2 [GATE] Confirm the discovery rule — no answer about a specification without knowing whether anything has been discovered since it was written.
· 8.6 [GATE] Your conflict resolution on your projects — national guidance vs officer requirement, consent conditions vs specification, archival record vs physical evidence, conservation vs structural necessity.
· 8.7 [GAP] Partial-evidence disclosure wording, and which decisions must never be offered as estimates — only cited exactly or refused.

9 — Never-estimate

· 9.1 [GATE] Which figures / decisions your organisation treats as absolute — consent requirement and scope, mortar type and mix per element, maximum permissible repair strength, vapour permeability requirements, whether element is original/later/conjectural, structural capacity of historic timber/iron/masonry, safe loading during temporary works, fixings into historic fabric, cleaning method and parameters per stone, consolidant selection, lime working temperature limits, species constraints, archaeological trigger depths, fire strategy for historic interior.
· 9.2 [GAP] Which of the above have caught you out, and how.

10 — May-estimate

· 10.1 [GAP] Figures you accept as estimates on your projects — programme duration, cost per m² repointing, quantity of stone replacement — and the exact wording you want attached.
