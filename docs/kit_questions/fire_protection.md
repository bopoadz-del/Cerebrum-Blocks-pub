# Fire Protection — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · building / system · code edition · source · date

1 — Hazard classification

· 1.1 [GATE] Your occupancy / area list with the actual hazard classification for each, and the named engineer or approved document it is attributed to.
· 1.2 [GATE] Who classifies on your projects — designer, AHJ, insurer, combination — and who can overrule whom.
· 1.3 [GAP] What triggers reclassification on your projects — tenant change, commodity change, rack height, process change — and how it is captured after handover.
· 1.4 [GATE] For storage: your commodity class, rack configuration, height, clearance, encapsulation — the design inputs as installed.
· 1.5 [GAP] Where the insurer requirement exceeds code on your projects, which governs, and where that is recorded.
· 1.6 [GAP] Which areas in your buildings are specially designated (high-piled storage, flammable liquid rooms, battery rooms, kitchens, atria, data halls) and why.

2 — System catalogue

· 2.1 [GATE] Your systems by building — which selected by code, which by insurer, which by client preference.
· 2.2 [GATE] For each gaseous system you have: design concentration, safety margin, room integrity requirement, and what invalidates the design after installation.
· 2.3 [GAP] Which systems you have with discharges that endanger occupants, and the pre-discharge / lockout / evacuation arrangements as practised on your sites.

3 — Cost

· 3.1 [GATE] Your per-m² costs per system — sprinkler by hazard, ESFR, in-rack, pump set, tank per m³, gaseous per m³, detection, smoke control, fire stopping per penetration, structural protection, commissioning, annual ITM, fire watch per hour.
· 3.2 [GATE] Your impairment cost set — fire watch, insurance loading, cover suspended, operations halt, hot work stop, occupancy withdrawal, emergency callout.
· 3.3 [GAP] Cost of failing an AHJ inspection or insurer survey on your projects — remedial, re-inspection, occupancy delay.
· 3.4 [GAP] Cost of a delayed certificate of occupancy when fire systems are the last item on the critical path.
· 3.5 [GAP] Cost of a false alarm / unwanted activation on your projects — evacuation, business interruption, water damage, brigade charges.

4 — Hydraulic design

· 4.1 [GATE] Per system type: your design density, design area, area per sprinkler, spacing, K-factor, C-factor, elevation losses, hose stream allowance, total demand, margin over supply, duration, storage volume — with the code edition each is drawn from.
· 4.2 [GATE] How you establish the hydraulically most demanding area — assumed, single-configuration, or multiple configurations analysed — and which areas you have actually analysed.
· 4.3 [GATE] Your required margin between demand and available supply, and its source — code, insurer, or practice.
· 4.4 [GATE] Your water supply basis per building — flow test, authority statement, tank and pump, or assumption. Date of the flow test and validity period.
· 4.5 [GAP] Your hose stream allowance value and where it is added — point of connection or base of riser.
· 4.6 [GAP] Which obstruction and clearance rules are most often violated on your projects by other trades after the design is complete.

5 — Life safety and egress

· 5.1 [GATE] Per building: occupant load, required exit capacity, number of exits, common path and total travel distance, dead end limits, exit width, stair capacity, refuge areas, assisted evacuation provisions — and who owns each calculation on your projects.
· 5.2 [GATE] For performance-based buildings: your accepted ASET/RSET margin, your tenability criteria, and who approved them.
· 5.3 [GATE] Which of your buildings rely on a fire engineered solution rather than prescriptive compliance, and what that solution assumes must remain true for the life of the building — and how that assumption is protected.
· 5.4 [GAP] Your firefighting provisions per building — access roads, hardstanding, risers, firefighting shafts, lifts, hydrant proximity — and which are most often compromised by later works.

6 — Commissioning, ITM and system state

· 6.1 [GATE] Per test — hydrostatic, flushing, main drain, pump acceptance, trip test, room integrity, cause-and-effect, integrated, smoke control, fire stopping — your acceptance criteria, who witnesses, and where the record lives.

· 6.3 [GATE] Your ITM intervals as practised — for each system, what is inspected / tested / maintained and at what frequency.
· 6.4 [GAP] What happens on a failed test on your projects — impairment, deficiency, or non-conformity — and who is notified, within what time.
