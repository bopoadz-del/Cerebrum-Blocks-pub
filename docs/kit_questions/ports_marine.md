# Ports & Marine — Questions I Cannot Answer

Answer format: value · unit · asset / berth · source document · revision · date

Section A — Figures that must never be guessed

· A.1 [GATE] Quay / berth structural limits — your quay wall design load (kN/m²), deck loading limits, any areas with reduced capacity, and the design dossier or as-built that holds them.
· A.2 [GATE] Declared depths and tidal windows — your berth depths, chart datum basis, latest hydrographic survey date and by whom, and any berths with seasonal or conditional depth.
· A.3 [GATE] Crane rail and wheel loads — your crane rail loading limits (kN/m), wheel load limits, and the crane/quay design documents that state them.
· A.4 [GATE] Bollard and fender capacities — your bollard safe working loads (tonnes), fender energy absorption and reaction limits, and the certification for each.
· A.5 [GATE] Container stacking and wind limits — your stack height limits by tier and by area, wind speed thresholds that trigger action, and where these are written.
· A.6 [GATE] Mooring analysis limits — your allowable mooring line loads, environmental limits for mooring, and any berth-specific restrictions.
· A.7 [GATE] Dredging tolerances — your permitted dredge depth, tolerance, and the regulatory or design basis.
· A.8 [GATE] Any other figure on your terminal where a wrong value causes structural failure, grounding, mooring failure, or toppling — list them with their authoritative source.

Section B — Figures that may be estimated

· B.1 [GAP] Your list. Typical candidates: berth productivity rates, expected turnaround times, seasonal congestion, planning-level vessel sizes. Confirm which you accept as estimates, and the disclosure wording.

Section C — Questions the system must refuse outright

· C.1 [GATE] Your refusal list. Likely candidates: is it safe to berth this vessel, can this crane lift this load, is this berth available, is the depth adequate today, can we stack higher. State each and what the system should say instead.

Section D — Source authority and precedence

· D.1 [GATE] Your ranking, highest first — flag / port state regulation, classification society, terminal design dossier, as-built drawings, operating manual, berth-specific procedure, harbour master instruction, shift instruction. Your order.
· D.2 [GATE] Revision currency — how someone on your terminal knows a document is the current revision, and what happens when a superseded one is used.
· D.3 [GATE] Figures where a local or project-specific value overrides the standard on your terminal — list them.
· D.4 [GATE] Figures that must never be applied outside their specific context — berth, asset, vessel class, product — on your terminal.

Section E — Units and confusion traps

· E.1 [GAP] Your unit-confusion history — tonnes vs kN vs kips, metres vs feet, chart datum vs berth datum, TEU vs boxes, GMT vs local, MHWS vs LAT.
· E.2 [GAP] Any case where a decimal, comma, or thousands separator caused trouble.

Section F — What has actually gone wrong

· F.1 [GAP] A time a figure came from the wrong source or the wrong revision.
· F.2 [GAP] A time a number was right but applied to the wrong context (wrong berth, wrong crane, wrong vessel class, wrong tide state).
· F.3 [GAP] A time someone assumed a figure because it "looked normal".
· F.4 [GAP] What a junior marine engineer most commonly gets wrong in their first year on your terminal.

Section G — Authority and confirmation

· G.1 [GATE] Which questions require a named person's sign-off before the answer is acted on, on your terminal.
· G.2 [GATE] Who may accept a figure for use — by role, not name.
· G.3 [GATE] Figures requiring two independent sources before use on your terminal.

Section H — Regulatory anchors

· H.1 [GATE] Your rule-and-figure list against the regulation, standard, or manual that anchors each — ISPS, SOLAS, MARPOL, IMDG, local port state, classification society rules, terminal design standards, national port regulations. Give what you have; partial is fine.

Section I — The five questions

· I.1 [GAP] Five things you'd ask a system per shift on your terminal and trust completely.
